# 03 – AI with your own stuff

**Date started:** _when I begin_
**Status:** 🌱 planned
**Where:** the Claude app. Use the paperclip or "+" button to attach files.
**Time:** about 30–45 minutes

## Goal
Learn what Claude can do with **my** files (documents, photos and spreadsheets),
and how to check that its answers really come from the file.

## The big idea
Claude only knows what's in the conversation, its **context**. Attaching a file
puts the file's contents into the context, so Claude can read it. That's
how AI apps "know" about your documents: nothing is learned permanently;
the file is just part of the conversation.

## Before you start: privacy
Only upload things you're comfortable sharing with an online service. Skip
anything with passwords, bank or ID numbers, or other people's private details.
For this project, everyday things are perfect: a product manual, a recipe, a
public article, a photo of your fridge or garden.

## Experiment 1: A document (PDF)
Pick a PDF of a few pages or more, like a manual, a guide or a long article.
1. `Summarize this document in 5 bullet points for someone who hasn't read it.`
2. Ask a **specific** question whose answer is in it, then:
   `Quote the exact sentence from the document that supports your answer, and tell me the page.`
   Check the quote against the real document.
3. Ask something that is **not** in the document but sounds like it might be.
   Does it say "the document doesn't mention this", or answer from general knowledge
   without saying so?

**The lesson to look for:** summaries are fast, but ask for quotes and page numbers
so you can check them. Watch whether it separates "in the document" from "what I know".

## Experiment 2: A photo
Take a photo of something everyday: your fridge's contents, a plant, a busy shelf,
or a sign or menu in another language.
1. `Describe everything you can see in this photo.`
2. Ask it to *do* something with it: `Suggest a dinner I could make from this`,
   `What plant is this and how do I care for it?` or `Translate this sign.`
3. Ask it to **count** something exact (`How many jars are on the top shelf?`) and
   count them yourself.

**The lesson to look for:** AI can understand images surprisingly well, but exact
details like counting, small text and fine positions are where it slips.

## Experiment 3: A spreadsheet
Use any spreadsheet (.xlsx or .csv): a budget, a list of expenses, a workout log.
No data to hand? Ask Claude in a separate chat: `Make me a CSV of 30 days of
made-up household spending with columns date, category, amount`, save it, and
upload that.
1. `What are the main patterns in this data?`
2. `What's the total for each category?` Check one total yourself.
3. `Make a chart showing spending by category.`

**The lesson to look for:** for numbers, Claude can write and run small programs
to do exact maths. Notice whether it does (you may see it "analyzing" or
running code). Calculated answers are far more reliable than estimated ones.
